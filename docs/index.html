Energy-Based Vector Hysteresis Model in Magnetics

The energy-based model describes a magnetic material as a set of cells whose magnetisation is held back by dry friction. The applied field splits into a reversible part that magnetises the material and an irreversible part that is spent against pinning. Everything below is one state, drawn four ways — move a slider and watch all four react together.

1 · The model in six lines

  1. The field seen by cell k splits into a reversible part Hr,k (the block’s position — what actually magnetises it) and an irreversible part Hi,k (the spring stretch — what is spent on pinning).

  2. Coulomb friction: the pinning field can never exceed the pinning force κk. Below it the cell sticks; at it the cell slips and dissipates energy.

  3. The vector play operator: the state is dragged only as far as the boundary of the ball of radius κk. This one line is the whole memory of the material.

  5. A spectrum of pinning forces, superposed with weights ωk. One cell gives a kinked loop; several give a smooth one.

  6. Why “energy-based”: the reversible field is the derivative of a stored free energy ψ, the pinning field is the subdifferential of a dissipation pseudo-potential ρ. The friction bound is not a fitted rule — it falls out of ρ.

How to read the panel

Pull the hand (the applied field H) and each block follows on a spring. While the spring force stays under that block’s friction limit κk the block does not move at all: the cell sticks, its magnetisation is frozen, and the field you added went entirely into the spring. Push past the limit and the block slips, dragged along at exactly the friction force. Reverse the hand and the spring must be unloaded and reloaded the other way before anything moves again — that lag, and nothing else, is the hysteresis you see in the loop.

In two dimensions the friction limit is no longer a threshold on a line but a circle of radius κk. The applied field can move anywhere inside that circle for free; the state Hr,k is only dragged when the field pushes the boundary, and then only along the direction of the push. That is what makes the model a vector model rather than a scalar loop applied twice: rotating the field at constant magnitude still moves the state, and the magnetisation trails the field by an angle instead of a phase.

Things worth trying

  • Set every κk to zero and sweep: the loop collapses onto the anhysteretic curve. No friction, no memory.
  • Give one cell all the weight and sweep: a sharply kinked loop. Spread the weight over three different pinning forces and the kinks blend into a smooth, realistic loop.
  • Sweep to saturation, return to H·ê = 0 and read the table: what is left is remanence — blocks that never made it home.
  • Drag H slowly around a circle larger than the biggest κ: every state follows on a smaller circle, lagging behind — rotational hysteresis.

Quantities on this page are dimensionless: the applied field, the pinning forces κk and the anhysteretic scale (a or A) share one arbitrary field unit, and magnetisation is measured against the saturation Ms you set. The model is drawn with three cells; a real identification uses tens.
